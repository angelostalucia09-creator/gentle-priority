STRESS

Understanding Acute Stress

Based on research from the American Psychological Association

What Is Acute Stress?

According to the American Psychological Association (APA), acute stress is the most common form of stress. It comes from demands and pressures of the recent past and anticipated demands and pressures of the near future.

It is short-term and often occurs when you face something new or challenging. In many cases, it can actually be beneficial — triggering your "fight-or-flight" response to help you handle a dangerous situation.

Common Triggers

  • Sudden changes — such as a job loss, relationship breakup, or moving home
  • Immediate physical danger or threat
  • High-pressure moments — public speaking, exams, or important deadlines
  • Unexpected events or emergencies

How It Feels

Acute stress symptoms appear quickly but usually don't last long:

  • Physical: Rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath, sweating, tense muscles, upset stomach
  • Emotional: Anxiety, irritability, feeling overwhelmed, fear
  • Mental: Racing thoughts, difficulty concentrating

Healthy Ways to Cope

  1. Take deep breaths: Try the 4-7-8 method — inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7, exhale for 8. This activates your body's relaxation response.
  2. Step back: If you can, remove yourself from the stressful situation temporarily.
  3. Talk it out: Share what you're feeling with someone you trust.
  4. Remember: This is temporary. Remind yourself that this feeling will pass.
  5. Move your body: A short walk can help release built-up tension.
💚 Note: Acute stress is a normal part of life. It's your body's way of protecting you. However, if these feelings persist or feel overwhelming, please consider speaking with a mental health professional.

Sources: American Psychological Association (APA), National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH)
