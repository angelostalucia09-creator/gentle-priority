Signs of Emotional Burnout

Based on research from the World Health Organization & Mayo Clinic

What Is Burnout?

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), burnout is a syndrome conceptualized as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It is characterized by three dimensions:

  • Feelings of energy depletion or exhaustion
  • Increased mental distance from one's job, or feelings of negativism or cynicism related to one's job
  • Reduced professional efficacy

Common Signs & Symptoms

The Mayo Clinic identifies these key signs of burnout:

Emotional Signs

  • Feeling cynical or critical at work
  • Irritability and impatience with others
  • Feeling emotionally numb or hopeless
  • Lack of satisfaction in achievements

Physical Signs

  • Feeling drained most of the time
  • Persistent headaches or muscle pain
  • Changes in sleep patterns — sleeping too much or too little
  • Changes in appetite

Behavioral Signs

  • Withdrawing from responsibilities
  • Procrastinating or taking longer to get things done
  • Using food, drugs, or alcohol to cope
  • Coming in late or leaving early

What You Can Do

Recovery from burnout takes time, but these steps can help:

  1. Set boundaries: Learn to say no and disconnect from work-related communications outside of hours.
  2. Seek support: Talk to trusted friends, family members, or a mental health professional.
  3. Prioritize sleep: Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep per night.
  4. Move your body: Regular physical activity can help you better manage stress.
  5. Take time off: Rest is not lazy — it's essential for recovery.
💚 Remember: Burnout is not a personal failure. It is a response to prolonged stress. Recognizing it early is the first step toward healing. If you're struggling, please reach out for professional help — you don't have to do this alone.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO), Mayo Clinic, American Psychological Association (APA)
