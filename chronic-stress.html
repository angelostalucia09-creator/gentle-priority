STRESS

Understanding Chronic Stress

Based on research from the National Institute of Mental Health

What Is Chronic Stress?

Unlike acute stress which comes and goes quickly, chronic stress lasts for a long period — weeks, months, or even years. The National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) explains that chronic stress occurs when the body experiences stressors that don't seem to end, such as ongoing poverty, an unhappy marriage, or a highly stressful job.

Because the body stays in "fight-or-flight" mode continuously, it can have serious effects on your physical and mental health over time.

Common Causes

  • Long-term poverty or financial hardship
  • Unemployment or job dissatisfaction
  • Chronic illness or injury
  • Unhappy marriage or family conflict
  • Workplace harassment or high-demand roles
  • Traumatic early-life experiences

Impact on Your Health

Research shows that long-term stress can affect nearly every system in your body:

  • Heart & Circulation: High blood pressure, increased risk of heart attack and stroke
  • Immune System: Weaker immunity — getting sick more often
  • Digestion: Stomach pain, bloating, and changes in appetite
  • Mental Health: Anxiety, depression, and difficulty concentrating
  • Sleep: Insomnia and poor sleep quality

How to Manage Chronic Stress

Healing from chronic stress is a process, but these steps can make a real difference:

  1. Identify the source: Understanding what's causing your stress is the first step toward change.
  2. Build a support network: Don't try to handle everything alone — connection protects mental health.
  3. Establish routines: Predictable daily habits — sleep, meals, exercise — create stability.
  4. Seek professional help: Therapy can provide effective tools. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is particularly well-researched and effective.
  5. Make small healthy changes: Even 10 minutes of daily movement, deep breathing, or journaling can help lower stress levels over time.
  6. Set boundaries: It's okay to say no. Protect your time and energy.
💚 Remember: Chronic stress is a health condition, not a character flaw. You don't have to "push through" it alone. Professional help is not a sign of weakness — it's a sign that you care about yourself and want to feel better. Healing takes time, and that's okay.

Sources: National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), American Psychological Association (APA), Mayo Clinic
