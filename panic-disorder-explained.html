Panic Disorder: Understanding Sudden, Intense Fear

Based on research from the American Psychological Association

What Is Panic Disorder?

Panic Disorder involves repeated, unexpected panic attacks — sudden surges of intense fear that peak within minutes. According to the American Psychological Association (APA), people with this condition often worry about when the next attack will happen and may change their daily habits to avoid situations they fear could trigger one.

What Does a Panic Attack Feel Like?

During an attack, you might experience:

  • Racing or pounding heart
  • Sweating and trembling
  • Shortness of breath or feeling smothered
  • Chest pain or discomfort
  • Dizziness or feeling faint
  • Numbness or tingling sensations
  • Fear of losing control or dying

Important Clarification

Having a panic attack does not mean you have Panic Disorder. Many people experience a single panic attack during times of extreme stress. Panic Disorder is diagnosed when attacks happen repeatedly — often without an obvious cause — and significantly impact your life.

What Helps

  1. Grounding techniques: Focus on your surroundings — name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, and 1 you can taste
  2. Slow breathing: Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 6
  3. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy: Teaches you that panic symptoms are uncomfortable but not dangerous
  4. Exposure therapy: Gradually facing feared situations in a safe way
  5. Medication: May be prescribed to help reduce the frequency of attacks
💚 Remember: Panic attacks feel overwhelming, but they are not dangerous. They are your body's alarm system going off when there isn't an emergency. With the right help, you can learn to recognize when an attack is coming and calm yourself down. You are safe, and you are not alone.

Sources: American Psychological Association (APA), National Institute of Mental Health, Mayo Clinic
