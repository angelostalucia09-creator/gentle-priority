Social Anxiety: More Than Just Shyness

Based on research from the National Institute of Mental Health

What Is Social Anxiety Disorder?

Social Anxiety Disorder is an intense, persistent fear of being watched and judged by others in social or performance situations. According to the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), it is different from shyness — it causes such intense fear that it interferes with daily life, school, work, and relationships.

Shyness vs. Social Anxiety

  • Shyness: Feels uncomfortable at first but fades quickly; doesn't stop you from doing what matters
  • Social Anxiety: Intense fear that lasts throughout the situation; you may avoid important events or endure them with extreme distress

Common Experiences

People with social anxiety may feel or experience:

  • Fear of speaking to strangers or in front of groups
  • Worry that others will notice your nervousness — blushing, sweating, trembling
  • Dread before social events, sometimes for days beforehand
  • Over-analyzing your performance after social interactions
  • Avoiding places where people will be watching you — eating, writing, or speaking in public

What Helps

  1. Gradual exposure: Facing feared situations in small, manageable steps — research shows this is one of the most effective ways to reduce anxiety
  2. Challenge your thoughts: Ask yourself: "Do I really know they're judging me? What's the evidence?"
  3. Focus outward: Instead of focusing on how you feel inside, focus on what others are saying
  4. Therapy: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy is highly effective for social anxiety
  5. Remember: Most people are focused on themselves, not on you
💚 Remember: You are not "too sensitive" or "overreacting." Social anxiety is a recognized mental health condition that responds very well to treatment. Many people who seem confident on the outside have struggled with this too — you are not alone. With patience and the right support, you can find your voice.

Sources: National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), American Psychological Association, Mayo Clinic
