Workplace Stress: Causes & Solutions

Based on research from the American Psychological Association & WHO

Understanding Workplace Stress

The World Health Organization (WHO) describes workplace stress as the response people have when presented with work demands and pressures that are not matched to their knowledge and abilities, challenging their ability to cope. It arises from a mismatch between what is expected of you and the resources you have to meet those expectations.

Common Causes

Research identifies these key contributors to workplace stress:

  • High workload: Unrealistic deadlines and consistently long hours
  • Lack of control: Having little say in how your work is done
  • Role ambiguity: Not knowing exactly what is expected of you
  • Poor workplace relationships: Conflict with colleagues or managers
  • Job insecurity: Concerns about losing your job or uncertain future
  • Work-life imbalance: Work demands spilling into personal time

Recognizing the Signs

You might notice:

  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Feeling irritable or short-tempered at work
  • Dread about going to work in the morning
  • Physical symptoms: headaches, fatigue, trouble sleeping
  • Decreased productivity or feeling less competent

What You Can Do

💡 At Work

  1. Set clear boundaries: Communicate your working hours and try not to check messages outside those hours.
  2. Prioritize tasks: Use the Eisenhower Matrix — separate what's urgent from what's important. Focus on one thing at a time.
  3. Take regular breaks: Step away from your workspace. Even 5–10 minutes can reset your focus.
  4. Speak up: If the workload is unmanageable, have a respectful conversation with your supervisor. Solutions often come from talking about the problem.

💚 Outside Work

  1. Create a transition ritual: Change clothes, take a short walk, or listen to music — something that signals "work is done."
  2. Make time for rest: Your worth isn't tied to your productivity. Rest is essential.
  3. Engage in non-work activities: Hobbies, exercise, and time with loved ones rebuild your energy.
💚 Remember: A job is just one part of your life. Your health — physical and mental — matters more than any deadline or task. If stress at work feels unmanageable and persists over time, it may be time to seek support or consider whether that environment is truly healthy for you.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO), American Psychological Association (APA), Mayo Clinic
